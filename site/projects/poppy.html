Poppy, explained

Eight step-through explainers of the Personal Agent Protocol (Poppy), draft 1.0. Each answers one question about how your personal AI agent deals with a Company on your behalf.

The first chapter’s answer: your agent signs every request to say who is asking, and you approve separately what it may do with your account. The other seven each take one part of the protocol in turn.

Step through chapter 1

The chapters

  1. How does Poppy work?

    Your agent signs every request to say who is asking, and you approve separately what it may do with your account.

  2. How does an agent know a poppy.json speaks for the shop?

    The domain names its issuer and the issuer lists the domain back, and each check stops one way to impersonate the shop.

  3. What is inside the seal?

    Three links: the agent's URL publishes its keys, one vouches for a short-lived key, and that key seals each request.

  4. How do you give your agent a key without giving it your password?

    Your password stays on the Company's own page; the agent gets a key cut to what you approved, revocable on its own.

  5. How does the agent's browser join the Session without ever carrying the key?

    The agent authorizes its browser's one-time code in a request of its own; the code works only with the cookie set in that browser.

  6. What does your agent do when the Company says no?

    Each refusal’s code names its own remedy, and the agent applies that one: the wrong one loops, or throws away a good token.

  7. When two agents talk, who is a person and who is an AI?

    Every message says whether a person or an AI wrote it, on both sides, and only your own agent can bring you in.

  8. What can a Company tell your agent, and what must your agent refuse to be told?

    What it won't do, other ways to do a task, and skills. Your agent takes all of it as advice, and only you give it orders.

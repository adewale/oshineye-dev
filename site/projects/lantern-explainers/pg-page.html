A one-integer Postgres row takes 36 bytes of an 8 KB page, and a bigint row takes the same

By default Postgres stores a table in pages of 8192 bytes, and every page starts with a 24-byte header. Two of its fields, pd_lower and pd_upper, mark where the free space starts and ends. On an empty page they are 24 and 8192, so 8168 bytes are free.

Transcript

Column: int4 · Fillfactor: 100 (default)

  1. An empty 8 KB page has 8168 bytes free

    By default Postgres stores a table in pages of 8192 bytes, and every page starts with a 24-byte header. Two of its fields, pd_lower and pd_upper, mark where the free space starts and ends. On an empty page they are 24 and 8192, so 8168 bytes are free.

    • An 8192-byte page drawn top to bottom, not to scale: a 24-byte header, then free space.
    • “An empty page”, 5 labelled values:
      • Page size: 8192 bytes;
      • Page header: 24 bytes;
      • pd_lower: 24;
      • pd_upper: 8192;
      • Free: 8168 bytes.
  2. One int4 row takes 36 bytes of the page

    An insert adds a 4-byte line pointer after the header and a tuple at the end of the page: a 23-byte header padded to 24, then the int4, 28 bytes. A 64-bit build stores each tuple in a multiple of 8 bytes, here 32, so a row costs 36 bytes.

    • An 8192-byte page drawn top to bottom, not to scale: a 24-byte header, then free space, then one 4-byte line pointer under the header and one 32-byte tuple at the very end of the page.
    • The 32-byte tuple in lines of 8 bytes, starting at offsets 0, 8, 16, 24: 23 header bytes, 1 padding byte, 4 data bytes, 4 padding bytes.
    • “pageinspect on Postgres 18.6”, 5 labelled values:
      • Tuple starts at, lp_off: 8160;
      • Tuple length, lp_len: 28;
      • Data starts at, t_hoff: 24;
      • pd_lower: 28;
      • pd_upper: 8160.
  3. A full page holds 226 int4 rows; 11.0% of it is data

    Inserts fill the page until the next row no longer fits: 226 rows at the default fillfactor. The pie splits the 8192 bytes, with each part’s bytes in brackets. The 226 tuple headers take 63.5%, more than the 11.0% that is the values.

    • Pie chart “One page of int4 rows”: 6 slices:
      • Row data: 904 (11.0%);
      • Tuple headers: 5198 (63.5%);
      • Alignment padding: 1130 (13.8%);
      • Line pointers: 904 (11.0%);
      • Page header: 24 (0.3%);
      • Free space: 32 (0.4%).
    • “int4, fillfactor 100”, 4 labelled values:
      • Rows: 226;
      • Bytes a row: 32 + 4 = 36;
      • Data a row: 4 bytes;
      • Overhead a row: 32 bytes.
  4. Row 227 would need 32 bytes and a pointer; 32 are left

    226 rows of 36 bytes use 8136 of the 8168 free bytes, leaving 32; pageinspect reports pd_lower 928 and pd_upper 960. Before an insert, hio.c asks for the tuple’s 32 bytes, and bufpage.c counts the free space less a 4-byte pointer: 28 bytes, too few.

      • Row data: 904 (11.0%);
      • Tuple headers: 5198 (63.5%);
      • Alignment padding: 1130 (13.8%);
      • Line pointers: 904 (11.0%);
      • Page header: 24 (0.3%).
    • “Why 226 rows”, 6 labelled values:
      • Free when empty: 8168;
      • 226 rows x 36 bytes: 8136;
      • Left: 32;
      • pd_lower, pd_upper: 928, 960;
      • Left less a pointer: 28;
      • Next row needs: 32.

    The same pie, edited to int8: the data goes from 4 to 8 bytes a row. Its 4 extra bytes take the place of the 4 bytes of padding that rounded the int4 tuple from 28 up to 32. The slices change size; the page still holds 226 rows.

  6. Fillfactor 100, the default, holds nothing back: 226 rows

    Back to int4. rel.h turns a table’s fillfactor into bytes an insert must leave free: 8192 x (100 - fillfactor) / 100, which is 0 at 100. So inserts fill the page to 226 rows. A table created with fillfactor 70 keeps 2457 bytes back and stops at 158.

      • Page header: 24 (0.3%).

      • Fillfactor: 100;
      • Held back: 8192 x 0%: 0;
      • Next row: 32 + 0: 32;
      • Free less a pointer: 28;
      • Rows a page: 226.
  7. int4 and int8 fill 45 pages alike; with text after them, 55 and 64

    Each line is a table of 10 000 rows at fillfactor 100, as measured on Postgres 18.6. int4 and int8 rows both take 36 bytes, so both tables take 45 pages. With an 11-character text column after the integer, the int8 table takes 64 pages to the int4 table’s 55.

  1. An empty 8 KB page has 8168 bytes free

    By default Postgres stores a table in pages of 8192 bytes, and every page starts with a 24-byte header. Two of its fields, pd_lower and pd_upper, mark where the free space starts and ends. On an empty page they are 24 and 8192, so 8168 bytes are free.

  2. One int4 + text row takes 44 bytes of the page

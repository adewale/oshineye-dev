Why is 0.1 + 0.2 not 0.3?

Ask JavaScript or Python for 0.1 + 0.2 and it prints 0.30000000000000004; ask whether that equals 0.3 and it says false. Nothing is broken: every language that uses the processor’s own floating-point numbers says false too. The numbers the machine adds are not quite the ones typed, and the sum is rounded once more.

  2. 0.1 is kept as a nearby fraction, a little high

    A double is a whole number over a power of two.

    A double, the machine’s number, holds a whole number divided by a power of two. One tenth is not such a fraction, just as one third has no finite decimal, so the machine keeps the nearest one it can: 3602879701896397 over 2 to the power 55, a little high. 0.2 is kept a little high and 0.3 a little low. Each decimal, and then their sum, is rounded to the nearest number the machine can hold.

  7. Whether a sum looks right depends on where it falls

    Four sums, what each prints as, and whether that is the decimal expected.

    Sums of decimals go through the same two roundings; what differs is where the exact sum falls between two doubles. 0.1 + 0.3 rounds onto the double for 0.4, 0.1 + 0.7 rounds to the double below the one for 0.8, and only 0.5 + 0.25 is exact, because halves and quarters are whole numbers over a power of two. So do not test sums of decimals with ==: compare within a tolerance, or count in whole units such as cents.

Check yourself

  1. Is 0.2 + 0.4 == 0.6 true or false, and what does the sum print as?

    Show the answer

    False: the sum prints as 0.6000000000000001. 0.2 is stored a little high and 0.4 a little high; their exact sum falls exactly halfway between the double 0.6 is stored as and the next one up, and the tie goes to the one whose last bit is 0, the upper.

  2. A loop adds 0.1 to a total ten times, starting from 0.1. Is the total 1?

    Show the answer

    No: it is 0.9999999999999999. Each addition rounds its exact result to a neighbouring double, and over the nine additions the small errors do not cancel. Count in whole numbers (ten tenths) or compare with a tolerance instead of ==.

  3. Why does 0.5 + 0.25 == 0.75 hold with no luck involved?

    Show the answer

    0.5, 0.25 and 0.75 are a half, a quarter and three quarters: whole numbers over a power of two, so each is stored exactly, their sum is a double already, and nothing is rounded.

Checked live, each time the model’s tests run, against CPython's float and this machine's JavaScript numbers, run by the model's tests.

How it was checked

CPython's own float, which model.test.ts runs through oracle/doubles.py each time: float.as_integer_ratio and decimal.Decimal for the exact fraction and every decimal digit of several hundred doubles, math.nextafter for their neighbours, repr for what each prints as, fractions.Fraction for the exact sum of two doubles, and float addition and float() for what the model's rounding must give. The same test holds the model's rounded sums and stored decimals to JavaScript's own + and Number() on this machine; answers.ts computes the check answers from the model.

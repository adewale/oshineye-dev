How does RTL change panels and field columns?

In a right-to-left page the reader starts at the right, so the card read first belongs there. This layout never says left or right: one card goes at start, the other at end, and the End card also sets its own field direction. On this rtl stage, which half will the Start card land in, and on which side of 123 will each card put its Field name?

Transcript

Stage direction: Right to left

  1. One layout on a right-to-left stage: which side is Start?

    Each card names a logical place, start or end, not a side of the page.

    In a right-to-left page the reader starts at the right, so the card read first belongs there. This layout never says left or right: one card goes at start, the other at end, and the End card also sets its own field direction. On this rtl stage, which half will the Start card land in, and on which side of 123 will each card put its Field name?

    • Code, 4 lines:
      • 1: stage(this, { direction: 'rtl' });
      • 2: s.card(rows, { at: 'start' });
      • 3: s.card(rows, { at: 'end',
      • 4: direction: 'ltr' }).
  2. Start lands on the right, its Field name right of 123

    The End card’s own ltr puts its Field name on the left.

    The rtl stage maps start to the right half and end to the left. The Start card also takes rtl columns from the stage, so its Field name sits to the right of the value, while the End card’s ltr puts its name on the left. Both still read 123: the columns swap sides, the digits inside a field do not. Start and End follow the stage’s direction, and a card’s field columns follow the stage unless the card sets its own.

    • “Start, rtl”, 1 labelled value:
      • Field: 123.
    • “End, own ltr”, 1 labelled value:
      • Field: 123.
  3. Changing a value does not reorder the fields

    Both values become 456; the columns stay put.

    Both cards change 123 to 456. The Start card keeps Field on the right and the End card keeps it on the left, because each card keeps the column direction it was built with.

    • “Start, rtl”, 1 labelled value:
      • Field: 456 (updated).
    • “End, own ltr”, 1 labelled value:
      • Field: 456 (updated).
  4. A card placed at left stays on the left

    Same rtl stage; these cards name sides, not logical places.

    Placed at left and at right, the cards keep those halves on this rtl stage, where start was the right. Use start and end for panels that should follow the reading direction, and left or right for something that must keep its side, such as a flowchart whose arrows run left to right. Both cards still take rtl columns from the stage. These cards hold Latin labels and digits; the page does not show how Arabic or Hebrew text is shaped.

      • Field: 123.

Check yourself

  1. On the rtl stage, add a third card at end with no direction of its own. Which half does it go to, and which side of its value is its Field name?

    Show the answer

    The left half, because end is the left on an rtl stage. With no direction of its own it takes rtl columns from the stage, so its Field name sits to the right of its value.

  2. A flowchart whose arrows run left to right must sit on the left half of the page in both reading directions. Should it be placed at start, at end or at left?

    Show the answer

    At left. Start and end swap halves when the stage direction changes; left keeps its side in both, as the last step shows.

  3. Give the End card direction rtl instead of ltr. On the rtl stage, how do the two cards’ fields compare?

    Show the answer

    They match: both cards now have rtl columns, so both put Field to the right of 123. Neither card reverses the digits.

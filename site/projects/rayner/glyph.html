Why does the same text take different space?

At size 0.9, “Hello, outlines” is 6.02 units wide in Inter and 8.10 in JetBrains Mono. The same characters start from the same left edge at the same size. The rulers disagree because the fonts assign different spacing. Which letters account for most of the difference?

Transcript

Embedded font: Inter

  1. Same words, same size: 6.02 or 8.10 units wide

    Which letters make up the 2.08-unit difference?

    At size 0.9, “Hello, outlines” is 6.02 units wide in Inter and 8.10 in JetBrains Mono. The same characters start from the same left edge at the same size. The rulers disagree because the fonts assign different spacing. Which letters account for most of the difference?

    • Hello, outlines. Inter. 6.02. Hello, outlines. JetBrains Mono. 8.10.
  2. Each glyph moves the pen on by its own advance

    Boxed in both rows: Inter’s narrowest letters, l, l, l, i, and its widest, H.

    Width is the sum of the glyphs’ advances, and each font sets its own; the gap between two ticks is one advance. JetBrains Mono gives all 15 characters 0.540, so its line is 15 × 0.540 = 8.10. Inter gives each l and i only 0.218: those 4 letters supply 1.29 of the 2.08-unit difference, and the comma, space and t 0.81, the space without drawing any ink. Only the H is wider in Inter, giving back 0.13.

      • l, l, l, i: +1.288 (marked);
      • comma, space, t: +0.813;
      • H: −0.129;
      • the other 7: +0.112.
    • 4 labels:
      • Hello, outlines;
      • Hello, outlines;
      • 6.0152 units · Inter;
      • 8.1000 units · JetBrains Mono.
  3. To fill its wider cell, JetBrains Mono draws a different l

    Will shape matching pair any Inter glyph with a JetBrains Mono one?

    The box round each l marks its advance, the width written under it. JetBrains Mono gives it more room and draws a flag and a foot; Inter uses a simpler stem. The character is the same, but its outline, the closed path that makes its ink, has changed. A shape matcher ignores position and size, so the outlines themselves must supply the correspondence.

    • l. Inter. advance 0.218. l. JetBrains Mono. advance 0.540.
  4. Answer: across the two fonts, none of the 14 glyphs pair

    Every glyph pairs with its 60% copy; with the other font, none do.

    Rayner’s shape matcher, TransformMatchingShapes, compares outlines after removing position and size. The 60% copy keeps those paths, so all 14 of its glyphs pair with the originals; the space has no outline to pair. In this phrase the other font changes the paths: none pairs across the fonts. A character name identifies a letter, not a particular outline.

Check yourself

  1. A space adds no outline. Why can adding one still move the next word farther right?

    Show the answer

    A space has an advance even though it draws no ink. The pen moves by that advance before the next glyph, so the line grows without another outline.

  2. “illicit” and “mammoth” both have seven letters. Set each in JetBrains Mono instead of Inter: which one grows, and could the other shrink?

    Show the answer

    In JetBrains Mono both are 7 × 0.540 = 3.78 units. “illicit” is mostly thin stems that Inter gives 0.218 each, so it grows from 1.90 to 3.78. Inter gives m 0.788, more than JetBrains Mono’s cell, so “mammoth” shrinks from 4.24 to 3.78.

  3. Could moving or uniformly resizing the Inter glyphs make them match the JetBrains Mono paths that failed to pair?

    Show the answer

    No. The matcher already removes position and uniform size before comparing paths. Those operations cannot remove differences in the outlines themselves.

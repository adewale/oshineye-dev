A new 1 TiB ext4 filesystem lets users write 93% of it; the 5% root reserve is nearly three times all its metadata

mke2fs.conf sets 4096-byte blocks, so 1 TiB is 268,435,456 blocks in 8,192 groups of 32,768. Before any file is written, 4,495,258 blocks, 1.7%, already hold metadata: the filesystem’s own structures. The pie counts blocks as dumpe2fs reports them after a real mkfs.ext4 run. The question is how much of this new disk a regular user can write: metadata consumes blocks now, while the root and kernel reserves hold back some of the remaining free blocks.

  2. Inode tables are 97.0% of the metadata

    One 256-byte inode per 64 KiB of disk: 335,544,320 inodes.

    This pie’s whole is the 21,625,887 metadata blocks. mke2fs makes one inode per 16 KiB of disk from 512 MiB to under 4 TiB, per 32 KiB to under 16 TiB, and per 64 KiB above. 20 TiB gets 335,544,320 inodes of 256 bytes, in tables set aside now: 20,971,520 blocks, 0.39% of the disk.

  3. The journal stops growing at 1 GiB

    It doubles with disk size from 16 MiB to 1 GiB; here 0.0049% of the disk.

    mke2fs sizes the journal in steps of disk size, doubling from 16 MiB at 128 MiB to 1 GiB at 128 GiB, and no larger after that. 20 TiB gets 262,144 blocks, 1 GiB: 1.2% of the metadata and 0.0049% of the disk.

    Superblock copies in 25 of 163,840 groups; two bitmaps in every group.

  5. The default 5% reserve is 268,435,456 blocks, 12.4 times the metadata

    The kernel holds back 4,096 more blocks; both come out of free blocks.

    Without -m, mke2fs reserves 5% of the blocks for root: 268,435,456. Its manual gives two reasons: less fragmentation, and root’s daemons keep working after other users are stopped. Once mounted, the kernel holds back 4,096 more blocks, 0.000076%, too thin to draw. Both come out of the free blocks.

  6. Users can write 94.6% of 20 TiB: 5,078,643,681 blocks

    statfs f_bavail: the free blocks less the root and kernel reserves.

    A user other than root can allocate blocks until only the two reserves are left: 5,347,083,233 - 268,435,456 - 4,096 = 5,078,643,681. statfs on the mounted filesystem reports exactly that as f_bavail, which df shows as Avail.

  7. With -m 0, users can write 99.6%

    Only the kernel’s 4,096 blocks are held back; users gain 1 TiB.

    mkfs.ext4 -m 0 reserves nothing for root, so only the kernel’s 4,096 blocks are held back. Users can write 5,347,079,137 blocks, 268,435,456 more than at the default, 1 TiB. Root then gets no more room than other users.

  8. At -m 0, users can write 93.5%, 98.3%, 99.6% of 1 GiB, 1 TiB, 20 TiB

    Metadata falls from 5.0% to 1.7% to 0.40% of the disk as disks grow.

    Metadata shrinks as a share of the disk, from 5.0% to 1.7% to 0.40%, because the journal stops at 1 GiB and larger disks get fewer inodes per byte. With no root reserve, almost everything else is for users.

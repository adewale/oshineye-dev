Why build the page before readers open it?

The token-bucket page offers 1, 2, 4 tokens a second, the only rates its reader can choose. R10 succeeds in all three, so its caption says “R10 is accepted at every rate”. Would that sentence survive a reader typing 0.1?

  2. At 0.1 tokens a second, R10 finds 0.5 tokens and is refused

    The sentence holds at every rate the page built and fails at one it never built.

    At 0.1 tokens a second, R10 still needs a whole token. After five seconds only 0.5 tokens have come back, so R10 gets 429. The caption described the offered inputs, not arbitrary rates. Rayner builds every case before anyone reads it, so rayner check can test each one and the author can read it, and a reader can open only a case the build made.

    • The token bucket’s ten requests at 1 token a second, 2 tokens a second, 4 tokens a second: 3 refused, 2 refused, 1 refused; R10, outlined, is accepted at each, finding 4.0, 5.0, 5.0 tokens.
    • Added below the three offered rates: the ten requests at 0.1 tokens a second, not offered, where only 5 are accepted and R10 finds 0.5 tokens and is refused.
  3. Lantern, the engine Rayner was forked from, rendered frames on request

    A reader can choose only a rate the scene lists, and each listed rate is a case the build runs.

    Lantern’s Worker rendered requested scene times on demand. Rayner prepares the page and diagrams ahead of reading instead. A reader can choose only a rate the scene lists. That gives an author a finite set of outcomes to inspect, but publishing a prepared page still does not make an unchecked caption true for arbitrary inputs.

  4. 3 pod placements × 2 traffic policies make 6 cases to build

    Every combination of options is a case the build makes and rayner check tests.

    Prepared inputs have a cost: settings multiply. 2 policies combined with 3 pod placements produce 6 cases, and another setting multiplies that collection again. Finite review becomes more work as the number of combinations grows.

    • A table of the traffic-policy explainer’s 3 pod placements by its 2 policies, Local and Cluster: 6 cases, numbered 1 to 6.

    Diagrams are captured at build time, so the page runs no diagram renderer.

Check yourself

  1. On the token bucket page, R8 and R9 arrive together at 1 s. If its author moved R10 from 5 s to 1.5 s, at which offered rate would ‘R10 is accepted at every rate’ become false, and could a reader see the false sentence?

    Show the answer

    At 1 token a second. There R8 spends the one token that has come back by 1 s, so at 1.5 s the bucket holds half a token and R10 gets 429; at 2 and 4 tokens a second it is still accepted. A reader could see it only if the author published without reading that case, because 1 token a second is a case the build makes and the author can run and read.

  2. Someone shares a link to the token bucket page that ends in ?rate=0.1. What does the reader see, and do they meet the false sentence about R10?

    Show the answer

    The player looks for 0.1 among the rates the scene lists, finds none, and opens the default case, 2 tokens a second, with the notice “Unknown rate option: 0.1. Showing the default case.” The reader sees a case the author ran, where R10 is accepted; the 0.1 row exists only in this explainer.

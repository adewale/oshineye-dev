Why does one version of a tune sound off the beat?

Each rectangle is a note: left to right is time, height is pitch and width is how long it sounds. Both versions play C D E G E D C C on the same instrument at 96 BPM. Play A, then B. Does one of them seem to pull against the beat somewhere? Where?

Version B: Start two notes half a beat later

  1. Eight notes in one order: which version sounds off the beat?

    Listen to both. Predict: where does B pull against the beat?

    Each rectangle is a note: left to right is time, height is pitch and width is how long it sounds. Both versions play C D E G E D C C on the same instrument at 96 BPM. Play A, then B. Does one of them seem to pull against the beat somewhere? Where?

    • A. C. D. E. G. E. D. C. C. B. C. D. E. G. E. D. C. C. Left to right: time. Higher: a higher pitch.
  2. Count 1 2 3 4: the 1 that opens each bar is the strong beat

    Predict: in B, which note starts on the 1 of bar 2?

    Count along with the clicks: 1 2 3 4, then again. Each large 1 opens a bar and gets a different click. In A a new note starts on every click. At the start of bar 2 it is E, outlined in the picture. Which note starts there in B?

    • A. 1. 2. 3. 4. 1. 2. 3. 4. C. D. E. G. E. D. C. C. B. C. D. E. G. E. D. C. C. Large 1: the strong beat that opens each bar.
  3. In B, no new note starts on the 1 of bar 2

    Red outline: G, the note still sounding when bar 2 begins.

    B lengthens the first E, delaying G; shortening the E after G puts D back on beat 2. The shifted B sounds off the beat because its G starts halfway through beat 4 and holds across the bar line, so the 1 of bar 2 brings no new note. This shift from strong beats to weaker parts is syncopation.

    • A. 1. 2. 3. 4. 1. 2. 3. 4. C. D. E. G. E. D. C. C. B. C. D. E. G. E. D. C. C. G starts mid-beat 4 and holds over the 1.
  4. With every pitch C, B still skips the 1

    Each version keeps its own starts and lengths; only the pitches go.

    Could the climb to G be what sounds off? Each version keeps its own starts and lengths, but all pitches are now C. Red dots mark starts between beats. Listen again: with the climb to G gone, B still holds a note across the bar line instead of starting one on the 1. Removing the melody leaves the timing difference intact. The score shows that difference; how strongly you hear its pull can vary.

    • A. 1. 2. 3. 4. 1. 2. 3. 4. C. C. C. C. C. C. C. C. B. C. C. C. C. C. C. C. C. Every pitch C. Red dot: a start between beats.

Version B: Keep the same timing as A

  1. Eight notes in one order: which version sounds off the beat?

    Listen to both: in this case B is the control.

    Each rectangle is a note: left to right is time, height is pitch and width is how long it sounds. Both versions play C D E G E D C C on the same instrument at 96 BPM. Here B’s starts and lengths match A’s exactly; the shifted version of B starts two notes half a beat later.

    • A. C. D. E. G. E. D. C. C. B. C. D. E. G. E. D. C. C. Left to right: time. Higher: a higher pitch.
  2. Count 1 2 3 4: the 1 that opens each bar is the strong beat

    Predict: in B, which note starts on the 1 of bar 2?

    Count along with the clicks: 1 2 3 4, then again. Each large 1 opens a bar and gets a different click. In A a new note starts on every click. At the start of bar 2 it is E, outlined in the picture. Which note starts there in B?

    • A. 1. 2. 3. 4. 1. 2. 3. 4. C. D. E. G. E. D. C. C. B. C. D. E. G. E. D. C. C. Large 1: the strong beat that opens each bar.
  3. E starts on the 1 in both versions; nothing crosses the bar line

    Red outline: G, which ends exactly as bar 2 begins.

    Here B has A’s starts and lengths. G ends at the bar line and E starts on the 1, so this control has no shifted starts. The shifted B sounds off the beat because its G starts halfway through beat 4 and holds across the bar line, so the 1 of bar 2 brings no new note. Only that version starts G late enough to hold it across the bar line.

    • A. 1. 2. 3. 4. 1. 2. 3. 4. C. D. E. G. E. D. C. C. B. C. D. E. G. E. D. C. C. G ends at the bar line; E starts on the 1.
  4. With every pitch C, A and B play one rhythm

    Each version keeps its own starts and lengths; only the pitches go.

    Every note now plays C, and each version keeps its own starts and lengths; a dot under each note marks where it starts. With the pitches gone, A and B play the identical rhythm, every start on a beat. Choose “Start two notes half a beat later” to hear a note hold across the bar line.

    • A. 1. 2. 3. 4. 1. 2. 3. 4. C. C. C. C. C. C. C. C. B. C. C. C. C. C. C. C. C. Every pitch C. Blue dot: a start on a beat.

Check yourself

  1. A version C starts from A, cuts the first D to half a beat and holds the E after it for one and a half beats, so G is back on beat 4. Which beat now brings no new note, and does any note cross the bar line?

    Show the answer

    Beat 3 of bar 1: E starts halfway through beat 2 and is still sounding on beat 3. Nothing crosses the bar line, so the 1 of bar 2 brings E, as in A.

  2. Count B in threes instead, 1 2 3 1 2 3, so a bar line falls after every third beat. Which note now holds across a bar line, and does the 1 of bar 3 bring a new note?

    Show the answer

    The first E. It starts on beat 3 of bar 1 and lasts one and a half beats, so it is still sounding when bar 2 begins, and G starts half a beat into bar 2. The 1 of bar 3, the seventh beat, brings a C that starts exactly there.

  3. Suppose the E after G lasted a full beat, as in A, with every later note following straight after it. Which notes of bar 2 would start on a beat?

    Show the answer

    None. D and both Cs would each start half a beat late, halfway through beats 2, 3 and 4, and the last C would run half a beat past the end of bar 2. Cutting that E to half a beat is what lets B land back on the beat.

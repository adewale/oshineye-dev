Why do balls dropped through pegs pile up in a bell shape?

A Galton board is a triangle of pegs over a row of bins. A ball dropped at the top hits a peg and falls to its left or its right, as likely one way as the other, then meets another peg. After six pegs it drops into one of seven bins. This ball went right 3 times and left 3 times, so it ends in the middle bin: a bin is the number of rights.

  3. The middle bin gets 20 balls, each edge bin 1

    More routes lead to the middle bins than to the edges.

    No ball was steered. Each of the 64 routes carried one ball, and 20 of those routes end in the middle bin while only 1 ends in each edge bin. More routes lead to the middle bins than to the edges, and since every route is equally likely, a ball is 20 times as likely to finish in the middle as at the far left.

    The answer is “The middle bin”.

  6. At the edges the curve drifts from the count

    The board ends at its last bin; the curve runs on past it.

    The curve is an approximation, and the edges show it. One route in 64 reaches the left edge bin, 1.56% of the balls; the curve gives that bin 1.85%. The curve also keeps going past the last bin, putting 0.21% of its area on each side where the board has no bins at all. Use the curve for the middle of a pile like this, and count routes at its edges.

Check yourself

  1. A smaller board has four rows of pegs, and one ball goes down every route. How many balls land in the middle bin, and why that many?

    Show the answer

    6 of the 16. A route to the middle bin turns right at two of the four pegs, and there are 6 ways to choose which two; only 1 route, all lefts, reaches the left edge.

  2. On the six-row board a ball turns left, right, right, left, right, left. Which bin does it land in, and how many other routes end there?

    Show the answer

    The middle bin: it turned right 3 times, and a bin is the number of rights. 19 other routes end there, each with the same three rights in another order.

  3. Someone bends every peg so that a ball goes right three times in four. The routes are the same 64. Does the middle bin still collect the most balls?

    Show the answer

    No. The number of routes to each bin is unchanged, 20 to the middle, but the routes are no longer equally likely: every right is three times as likely as a left, so a route with more rights is more likely. The bin one from the right edge now collects the most, 35.6% of the balls, and the middle bin 13.2%.

How it was checked

Python's standard library, which model.test.ts runs through oracle/galton.py each time: math.comb for the number of routes to every bin of boards with 0 to 12 rows, fractions.Fraction for each bin's chance when a peg sends the ball right three times in four, and statistics.NormalDist for the bell curve's height and areas; answers.ts computes the check answers from the model. No physical board was measured.

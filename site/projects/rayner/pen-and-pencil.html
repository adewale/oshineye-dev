How a hand-drawn timeline keeps its labels inside fixed boxes

A simulated hand makes every stroke a little different. If it also decided where the words and boxes went, the timeline could lose its structure. This example starts with four named periods and no positions; the next steps lay out their boxes before drawing them by hand. The pele page uses the same separation for four charts, though this timeline is our own model, not its code.

  4. The hand strays at most 0.002 units from the line it follows

    A damped spring chases the line; tremor shakes it.

    The pen is a mass on a damped spring, 8 Hz with damping 0.6, chasing a point that moves along the line at 1.2 units a second, shaken by tremor of up to 0.006. Close on the Midnight marker, the spring smooths the tremor into slow wobbles about the blue ring.

  6. The chart is drawn last, then its words

    Objects, then words; no ink strays over 0.005 from its mark.

    Periods become planks, events become tags hung on strings, and the rail becomes a rope: 13 objects drawn on the laid-out marks, no ink more than 0.005 units from them. Hershey letters come last, inside the boxes the layout gave; a second drawing changes the strokes and scenery without moving the chart’s laid-out boxes.
